I once told a student the strongest move in a round was conceding a point cleanly, and she looked at me like I'd asked her to lose on purpose. Eighteen years coaching debate at a public high school in Newark, and half the job is unteaching the idea that disagreement is combat. I also teach eleventh-grade U.S. history, which is the same work in different clothes - both ask you to sit inside someone else's case long enough to find its load-bearing wall.

## Where I Actually Live

Tuesday and Thursday afternoons, our team crowds into a room built for twenty-two desks and holds thirty. I've watched kids who couldn't get through a hallway conversation without flinching learn to stand and dismantle a resolution on foreign aid with real precision, then shake hands and mean it. That shift is why I stayed in this building instead of chasing a district with a bigger budget.

## The Long Argument With Myself

I grew up thinking winning meant the other person left smaller. Coaching cured me of that case by case, the way water cures stone. Now I think the clearest attention you can pay someone is to argue with them properly - full effort, no letting them win to be gentle. I'm curious how other fields hold that discipline: courtroom mentors, union negotiators, anyone who has to disagree for a living without wrecking the relationship. What I want next is reach beyond the tournament circuit, and a sparring partner who reads history differently than I do.
