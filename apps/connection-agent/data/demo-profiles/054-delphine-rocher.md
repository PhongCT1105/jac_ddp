Three years in, and I still haven't been allowed to build a formula of my own. Most mornings I stand in front of unlabeled vials and name them blind — oakmoss, galbanum, a rose absolute versus a cheaper stand-in pretending to be rose. My teacher calls this the boring decade. I call it learning to read before anyone lets me write a sentence.

The work has rearranged how I move through the world. I catch rain before it falls, and I've learned to distrust my own nose on tired days, when everything flattens into one beige note.

What the blind-naming drills keep hammering into me:

- Naming a smell correctly and understanding why it moves someone are different skills entirely — the second one takes years, not a longer vocabulary list.
- Cheap materials aren't lesser materials — the best effects often come from something plain doing unexpected work beside something expensive.
- Don't trust a scent you "recognize" until you can name three specific things it's built from. Memory misleads here more than anywhere else.
- Patience isn't passive waiting; it's repeated attention until familiar materials stop being generic and become individual.

What I want most is company more than advice — people years into something slow and unglamorous, who'll describe the apprenticeship honestly. I'm also curious about anyone thinking hard about memory, categorization, or how expertise gets built through repetition.
