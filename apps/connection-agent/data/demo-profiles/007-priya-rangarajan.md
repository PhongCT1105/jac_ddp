Most weeks I sit with people while they say the things they've been saving for last, in a hospice ward in Chennai where the corridor smells of jasmine and antiseptic in equal measure. Thirty-eight now, and I've learned grief has its own grammar — silences that outweigh words, hands that ask to be held without asking. What keeps me upright afterward is absurd, over-plotted comic novels, the kind where nothing matters and that's the point. I'm curious how people build a pressure valve into work that never lets up, and about dying well across households. The conversation I actually want happens near midnight, when the ward has gone quiet and neither of us is performing composure anymore.
