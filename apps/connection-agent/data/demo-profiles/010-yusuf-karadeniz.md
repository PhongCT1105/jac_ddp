Hand me a kilim and I can usually tell you within a decade when it was dyed — a madder red goes different once chemical dyes crept in, a walnut brown from certain villages near Sivas never bleached the same way twice. Fifty-two years of this, mostly reweaving what moths and bad storage undid, matching wool that isn't spun the old way anymore. What keeps me up isn't the work, it's that almost nobody under thirty asks to learn it, and the eye for a re-dyed patch doesn't transfer through photographs. What I can't solve: no one spins wool the old way — undyed, right twist, some lanolin left — and substitutes fail under raking light within a year. A mill with old records would be a real lead.
