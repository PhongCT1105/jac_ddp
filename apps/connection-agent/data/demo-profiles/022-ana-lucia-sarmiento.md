I keep a shotgun mic wrapped in a scarf that used to smell like my grandmother's kitchen, and that's the closest thing I have to a founding belief: sound reaches me before image does. Most of my paid work is boom and location sound for other people's documentaries around Bogotá. The unpaid work is mine.

## The Buildings That Are About to Stop Existing

When a demolition notice goes up, I try to get inside before the crews do. Not for drama — a building has a last acoustic signature and then it's gone for good. I've caught the reverb of a shuttered textile mill in Fontibón, a pharmacy on its final week, a theater whose ceiling fans still turned in an empty house. I don't fully know what the archive is for yet. Maybe a score for a film that doesn't exist. Maybe just a refusal to let a place go quiet without someone noticing.

## Why the Microphone Is Not a Neutral Object

People treat sound as the role that doesn't matter until the mix falls apart, but the microphone is closer to a confessional than a camera — subjects forget it's recording the breath under the composed sentence. I've walked off two shoots over how a director wanted testimony pulled out of someone. It cost me the fee. I'd do it again.

I'm looking for archivists, urban planners who read cities the way I listen to them, composers who want raw material instead of stock ambiance, and anyone patient enough to wait out a room until it finally goes quiet.
