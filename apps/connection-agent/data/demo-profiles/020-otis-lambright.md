There's a particular quiet when a first-year apprentice traces a fault on their own instead of asking me where it is, and I've spent six years chasing that quiet more than any paycheck. Twenty-some years pulling wire and bending conduit across Pittsburgh came before the local asked me to run training, and I assumed I'd miss the fieldwork. Mostly I don't. What holds my attention now is teaching someone to read a panel the way you'd read a room — which shortcuts get people hurt, which just get them yelled at. My opinion, and I'll defend it: apprenticeship should run a year longer, because judgment can't be rushed to hit a quota. Most tradespeople think I'm wrong. Convince me.
