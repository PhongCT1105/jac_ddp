Twenty-two years ago I swept hair off a linoleum floor for the privilege of touching a razor. Now I own the shop, and I still work the second chair from the window, because morning light tells you the truth about a fade faster than any mirror. Same three hundred square feet it was in 2003, but the men who sit in my chair have gotten looser with what they'll say out loud, and that shift is the part of this work I never expected to care about most.

## Second Chair, Best Seat
I could take the chair by the door, the one that greets everybody first. I wanted the one people ease into once small talk's already spent itself on somebody else. By the time a regular reaches me he's decided the room is safe. That's the real inventory I keep — not clippers and capes, but forty quiet minutes where a man might mention a father's funeral, a diagnosis, a marriage he's trying not to lose.

## What I'm Building Past the Chair
I want to make deliberate what's been happening by accident — a Sunday sit-down after close, maybe a partnership with someone who understands grief or fatherhood better than I guess at. I'm not a counselor and won't dress it up as therapy. I just know how to hold a room still enough that someone else can finally talk.

## Who I'd Like to Sit With
People who study how men talk, or why they don't. Other owners keeping something handmade alive in a city that keeps flattening into chains. Anyone who's turned an ordinary workplace into a refuge, and can tell me what worked.
