Off a side street near the river sits a back room with eleven thousand records sorted by a system only I can read, and most nights I pull the crate I pulled at twenty-six, the year a cumbia first ran past what the station wanted. Thirty-two years on, I'm still up after midnight, coaxing a warped pressing through the needle for callers who hum only the horn line they want. What occupies me now is the arithmetic of decay: tape, vinyl, the musicians who taught me the clave, fading faster than I can record them. I'd welcome an engineer to help save these reels, or anyone willing to argue which pressing is true.
