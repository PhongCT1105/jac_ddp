Seven years ago a bank sent a letter to a dairy that was about to close, and my friend Marc-André and I bought it instead of reading the letter twice. Neither of us had made cheese before. We had cows we knew by name, a barn with a bad roof, and a stubbornness that turned out to be the only ingredient that mattered.

What I actually run now, most days:

- a herd of forty-one, mostly Canadienne, the old breed nobody wanted until the milk got interesting again
- a curing room I rebuilt from a root cellar, humidity checked by hand twice a day
- a business partnership that has survived three near-bankruptcies and one very bad winter

The milk taught me chemistry. The partnership taught me everything else — how to disagree without ending something, how to let another person be wrong in front of a customer and fix it later, how trust behaves under debt. I read balance sheets now the way I used to read weather.

Sundays I drive the wheels to a market two hours south and talk to strangers about mold cultures until they buy something.

What I want next: someone who's rebuilt a failing thing with a partner and can tell me what breaks in year ten that didn't break in year three.
