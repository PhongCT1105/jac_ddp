The reservoir under Kralingen holds enough water to keep half the city drinking for two days if the main supply fails, and I think about that number more than is strictly healthy. I test compliance for the municipality here in Rotterdam — chlorine residuals, turbidity, the occasional scare over old lead service lines — and after twenty-two years I still corner strangers at dinner parties to explain why a water tower's shape is physics, not decoration. What I want now is people who find the unglamorous machinery of cities as gripping as I do: engineers, novelists researching infrastructure, anyone trying to rebuild public trust in a utility after it broke.
