Thirty years ago I caught my first baby in a hallway because the delivery room was full, and I never quite left that hallway in spirit. Most of my hours now go to the younger midwives at our public clinic in Enugu — teaching them to read a labouring woman's breathing before the monitor does, and to stay steady when three deliveries land at once. Watching one of them catch her first baby without me in the room has become its own reward. I wonder often how skill like this gets handed down when nobody has time to write it down, and I'd enjoy comparing notes with anyone training people for demanding, hands-on work under pressure.
