I've spent most of my adult life in break rooms — hospital cafeterias at 2 a.m., hotel laundry rooms that never quite cool down, waiting for someone to finish venting so the real conversation can start. Twenty years now, mostly with housekeepers, cooks, nursing aides, and hotel line staff across Los Angeles. I came to this work at twenty-two, upset about something specific I've since forgotten, and stayed because I turned out to be good at the part almost nobody wants: sitting still while a room decides whether to trust you.

## The label that never quite fits

People need a word for a woman who doesn't fold at the table, and the word they usually reach for is angry. I've sat through negotiations as the calmest person in the building, because by then I've already heard the same story from forty people and know which sentence will land. What actually moves me isn't volume — it's someone doing math on a surgery they can't afford, or a schedule that never lets them see their kid awake before dark. I don't raise my voice for that. I ask better questions.

## What I still want to get right

I'm curious how organizers outside my industries handle the parts nobody trains you for — burnout that doesn't announce itself until it's a resignation letter, or holding a room together after a loss. I'd like conversations with people who've built something slower and sturdier than a single contract fight, and with anyone willing to be interrupted mid-sentence and not take it personally.
