Four years ago I tore out most of the cross-fencing my father strung in 1968 and started over with polywire and a solar charger, moving the herd every two or three days instead of turning them loose on a pasture for the whole season. Half my neighbors figure I've gone soft or gone broke, probably both some weeks. I run cattle on dry, rolling country in eastern Montana, ground that punishes anyone who quits paying attention to it.

## Arguing With a Dead Man

My father ran this outfit for forty years and never once doubted set-stocking a pasture till it went gray. Every time I check the rain gauge or walk a paddock at dawn I'm having an argument with someone who can't answer back anymore. I keep bracing to feel like I've betrayed the place he handed me. Instead the grass comes back thicker each spring, the creek's got willows along it again, and some evenings that's the only vindication I need.

## Ground Truth Over Coffee

I've learned more from a soil probe and a dog-eared notebook than from any extension bulletin, though I'll still read the bulletin. What I'm after now is other people doing the unglamorous math on stocking rates, or quietly rebuilding something their family got wrong, or just willing to argue land management over coffee without pretending either of us has it solved.
