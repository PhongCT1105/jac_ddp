Liner notes for a record that doesn't exist yet. No label, no release date, just the running order I'd use to explain a life in tracks instead of sentences.

**01 — Basement, No Windows**
I was nineteen when I turned my mother's cave in Saint-Denis into a studio, mostly because the basement had a door that shut and the upstairs neighbors didn't have to hear it. Egg cartons on the walls for a year before I could afford real foam. I taught myself signal flow off forums and by ruining a lot of vocal takes. Twelve years later the room is still underground, and I still think the best ideas here come from places without a view to perform for.

**02 — Everyone Who Came Down the Stairs**
Free sessions for anyone under eighteen from the block, twice a week, since year three. No name on it, just an open door and a rule that whoever's there helps whoever's newer. I've watched kids arrive unable to look at the microphone and leave arguing about arrangement choices like veterans. Half of what I know about compression I learned by explaining it badly to a fifteen-year-old.

**03 — The Ledger**
The studio pays for itself through session work and a handful of sync placements I'm still quietly proud of. Every quarter a slice goes into a fund I call, only to myself, the next room, because the current space tops out at four people. I've been meeting with a lawyer about giving the whole thing a proper structure, board and all, instead of a favor economy that depends on me alone to hold it up.

**04 — Hands, Not Theory**
Drums first, always. I build a beat the way a mason lays brick — pattern, then weight, then the one deliberate crack that makes it breathe. I'm better at making a nervous voice sound confident than at anything else I do, and I don't fully understand why that skill lives in me, only that it does.

**05 — Interlude, Things I Didn't Finish**
An album under my own name, twice attempted, twice shelved, because I kept hearing it as somebody else's session and fixing it instead of finishing it. I'm making peace with being better at building rooms than standing in the middle of them.

**06 — Curiosities**
Lately I'm reading about how griot families passed down memory as structured performance long before anyone called it archiving, and it's changed how I think about what a recording preserves. I want to understand acoustics properly, the physics, not the folklore. I'm also caught up in how neighborhoods keep their sound through redevelopment, or don't.

**07 — Next Pressing**
A permanent, funded space with real hours and a second engineer, so the whole thing survives a bad month for me personally. Eventually, a small network of rooms like this one in other suburbs, run by people who came up through mine.

**Outro — Credits**
Recorded and mixed in the basement in Saint-Denis, on a rule that anyone under eighteen from the block gets the room for free. No label, no catalog number, just the stairs and whoever came down them. Thanks to every kid who showed up scared of the microphone and left running the session. This one still needs: a second engineer so the place survives a bad month, a board that doesn't depend on one person's signature, and a second room in another suburb to prove the first wasn't a fluke.
