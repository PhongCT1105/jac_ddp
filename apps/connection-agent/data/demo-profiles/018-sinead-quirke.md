Half past four and I'm elbow-deep in a calving outside Ennistymon, which is how you find me most weeks. I qualified as a veterinary nurse and gravitated to large animals — cattle, horses, the odd cranky donkey — because the patient can't tell you what's wrong and you earn the answer with your hands. Farmers here trust me with things they'd never say to a stranger. There's a figure growing quietly in an account nobody knows about, tied to a plan I'm not ready to name, and I get on with people who understand building something slow and private before it's real. I'd talk for hours with anyone who's done the same, or who wants to know how you set a fractured leg in a field with no lights.
