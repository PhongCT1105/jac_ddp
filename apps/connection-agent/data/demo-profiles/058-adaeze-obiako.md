Most mornings I am not at my desk, I am at Wakulima or Gikomba market, watching a tomato seller tap her phone when a transaction fails. Our payments product handles thousands of these moments daily, and the graphs say everything is fine. The traders tell a different story, usually with their hands. I built thirteen years of work on the gap between what a dashboard reports and what a person experiences, and I have stopped trusting the dashboard to close that gap alone.

Here is what survives contact with a trader's hands more reliably than my own dashboard ever does:

- A drop-off number never tells you whether someone gave up, got interrupted by a customer, or found a workaround worth stealing for the product.
- Trust in a payment tool is built in seconds of friction, not features; traders remember the one stall far more than fifty smooth transactions.
- The most useful thing I can do in a meeting is repeat exactly what a trader said, in her words, before anyone reaches for a solution.
- Cash habits are not irrational, they are evidence of a system that hasn't earned enough patience yet to be trusted with everything.

I want to get better at turning market-floor knowledge into decisions without flattening it into a slide. Here is the trade: show me where your instruments and your users disagree, and let me watch it happen. In return I bring thirteen years of translating hands into decisions, and the habit of repeating a user's words before anyone reaches for a fix.
