A courtroom has its own grammar, and after twenty years inside it I still catch new tenses. I move between Serbian, English, and German, and the third language is where the real work happens - not the words everyone agrees on, but inat, or the particular hollow of nedostajanje, missing someone as an ongoing act rather than a state. Interpreting for asylum hearings taught me some silences need translating too. At 45 I am collecting the words that resist me into something longer than court notes, and inat is the one that never crosses whole. It shrinks into "spite," losing the pride that makes someone choose the harder path on purpose - and in court that missing pride can read as a lie.
