A courtroom has its own grammar, and after twenty years inside it I still catch new tenses. I move between Serbian, English, and German, and the third language is where the real work happens - not the words everyone agrees on, but inat, or the particular hollow of nedostajanje, missing someone as an ongoing act rather than a state. Interpreting for asylum hearings taught me some silences need translating too. At 45 I am collecting the words that resist me into something longer than court notes, and I want conversations with people building glossaries or arguing that some feelings just do not travel.
