There's a sound a bridge deck makes under a loaded truck that most people never register, but after twenty years I can tell you a lot from that thump and shudder. I used to draw buildings meant to be admired the day they opened; now I spend mornings in waders under the Volta crossings, tapping at concrete nobody looks at until it fails. Somewhere in that move from rooftops to riverbeds I started thinking of upkeep as its own kind of devotion, quieter than building but harder to walk away from. What do you take care of, quietly, that nobody notices until the day it fails — and how do you tell the difference between something merely aging and something actually starting to give way?
