The question that follows me up every scree slope is where exactly caution turns into cowardice, and where boldness turns into a body on a stretcher. I've spent eleven years asking it in a clinic and on call-outs above this valley, and I still don't have a clean answer, only better instincts.

### What the clinic teaches that the mountain doesn't

Rehab is patient arithmetic: a knee regains its trust in small, boring increments, and the people who heal fastest are rarely the toughest, just the most honest about pain. I track a hiker's gait the way I track a client's confidence after a fall, watching for the flinch that says the tissue is fine but the nerve hasn't caught up. That gap between what the body can do and what the mind will allow is where most of my real work happens now.

### On the ridge, past midnight

Search callouts strip the theory out of risk fast. You learn that recklessness is usually someone else's plan colliding with weather they didn't respect, while risk is a plan you chose with your eyes open. I'm drawn to people who've had to make that same distinction under pressure, whatever their field, and to anyone building better ways to teach judgment rather than just rules.
