The moment I trust most in any lesson is the second after a demonstration lands, when thirty fourteen-year-olds go quiet because a flame changed colour or a solid vanished into a solution they didn't expect. Four years into teaching chemistry in Leicester and that hush still matters to me more than any grade does. I keep a growing list of reactions safe enough for a school lab but strange enough to earn it, and I'll trade the whole list — plus the ones I've quietly benched as too risky — for a good account of how you engineer a single reaction out of a room, whatever your craft. Bring me a theory of whether wonder actually teaches anything, and I'll bring you the counterargument.
