A family bible came into the shop where I was interning with its spine cracked clean through, and something about resewing those brittle signatures back into a text block made three years of a graduate program feel beside the point. I left to apprentice properly, and now I run a small bindery in Portland, Oregon, where a nineteenth-century prayer book with someone's grandmother's marginalia might sit on the bench next to a thesis draft that needs a sturdy case before a defense. I'm teaching myself gold tooling slowly, on purpose. I'm tired of being asked if I've restored anything famous. I'd rather talk about keeping a slow trade solvent without turning it into nostalgia.
