Engine room log, unofficial entry: four days out from Fujairah, generator two running rough, wrote this between checks because the satellite window is short and I keep meaning to actually finish a profile instead of starting one.

Eleven years now on bulk and container ships, most recently as second engineer. I can diagnose a failing bearing by sound before the sensors flag it, keep a forty-year-old diesel plant alive with parts sourced in three languages, and sleep four hours at a stretch for weeks without losing my temper — mostly. What I'm less sure of is what any of that is worth on land. Contracts run four to six months, then Mumbai, then eleven weeks of leave I've spent, in order:

- reading everything my sister recommends
- half-starting a course in renewable energy systems
- realizing I don't actually know how to be somewhere for longer than a season

I like problems with grease on them and people who explain things precisely. I'm curious about shore-based engineering, teaching cadets properly instead of in stolen minutes, and whether the restlessness is the sea or just me. Interested in talking to anyone who's rebuilt a life after a rotating one, or who just wants the unglamorous truth about what the engine room actually sounds like at 3 a.m.
